Publications

  • [C1] PULSE: Socially-Aware User Representation Modeling Toward Parameter-Efficient Graph Collaborative Filtering (WWW 2026 Oral) [Paper | Code] Doyun Choi*, Cheonwoo Lee*, Biniyam, Taewook Ham, Chanyoung Park, Jaemin Yoo
    • Constraint: conventional graph collaborative filtering assigns explicit embeddings to every user and item, causing parameter growth and scalability issues under sparse or noisy social interactions.
    • Problem definition: incorporate socially meaningful signals into user representations while reducing the parameter and computational burden of graph recommendation.
    • Model: PULSE (Parameter-efficient User representation Learning with Social Knowledge), combining community-aware embedding generation (CEG), socially-connected item-aware embedding generation (SIEG), adaptive gated fusion, and a LightGCN backbone trained with BPR and self-supervised objectives.
    • Experiment and evaluation: compared against 13 graph collaborative filtering and social-recommendation baselines on Douban-Book, Yelp, and Epinions using Recall@K and NDCG@K, with additional evaluations under sparse-interaction, cold-start, and noisy-social-network settings; also measured parameter size and computational efficiency.
    • Improvement and impact: reduced parameters by up to 50% compared with LightGCN and improved NDCG@20 by 9.0%, 8.1%, and 8.5% over the best competitors across the three datasets.
    • Role and collaboration: contributed as a co-author spanning model development, experiments, and paper preparation; accepted as an Oral paper at WWW 2026.
  • [Under Review] Beyond Graph Information Bottleneck: Creating Bimodal Edge Score Distributions for GNN Explanation [Paper] Taewook Ham, Cheonwoo Lee, Myeong Kong, Jaemin Yoo
    • Constraint: graph explainers lack edge-level ground-truth labels and often produce continuous, ambiguous edge-importance scores even when their ranking metric is high.
    • Problem definition: improve explanation accuracy while making important and unimportant edges more clearly separable.
    • Model/method: BEEP (Bimodal Edge Explanation via Polarization), a self-guided framework that converts high-confidence edge scores into pseudo-labels and iteratively trains a guided explainer beyond the Graph Information Bottleneck formulation.
    • Experiment and evaluation: applied BEEP to existing post-hoc explainers, including both GIB-based and non-GIB approaches, and evaluated it on four synthetic and real-world benchmark datasets using AUC, F1, binarization, and bimodality metrics, complemented by ablation and robustness studies.
    • Improvement and impact: consistently improved predictive and distributional explanation metrics across baselines; on MNIST-75sp, the binarization score increased from 0.162 to 0.714, while the method maintained practical computational efficiency with only modest additional inference overhead.
    • Role and collaboration: led the master's thesis research, including problem definition, method design, theoretical analysis, implementation, experiments, and manuscript preparation; manuscript currently under review.
  • [Under Review] PNE-GAD: Self-Explainable Graph-Level Anomaly Detection via Normality-Evident Graph Representations Doyun Choi, Myeong Kong, Taewook Ham, Jaemin Yoo
    • Constraint: graph-level anomaly detection must identify abnormal structures while making the evidence behind each prediction inspectable.
    • Problem definition: develop a graph-level anomaly detector whose representations expose normality-related evidence rather than producing an opaque anomaly score only.
    • Model: PNE-GAD, a two-stage self-explainable graph-level anomaly-detection framework that uses a GIN-based Normality Explanation Detector (NED) with an MLP projection head, explanation prototypes, and a WL-kernel-based normality evidence signature for anomaly decisions.
    • Experiment and evaluation: evaluated PNE-GAD against 15 baselines across nine bioinformatics, molecular, and social-network datasets using AUROC, with two normal/anomalous class configurations, repeated runs with five random seeds, and additional ablation, contamination-robustness, and explanation-quality studies.
    • Improvement and impact: achieved the best anomaly-detection performance on 7 of 9 datasets and the best average rank across all datasets, while grounding each decision in interpretable normality evidence rather than an opaque anomaly score alone.
    • Role and collaboration: contributed to the four-author research collaboration on model experiments and analysis; manuscript currently under review.
